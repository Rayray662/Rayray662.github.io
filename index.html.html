Entry 01 : Where I'm From Kept by T. Nkosi

Born Into Two Lines

T'Kari was born into the tribe Zulu. In a community where strength was valued more was discipline and responsibility. He was one of the tribe's greatest warriors and his mother was keeper of their history and traditions.

It was expected that from a young age T'Kari would follow in his father's footsteps. But T'Kari never wanted to follow in his father's footsteps. He wanted to find out what went on in the world beyond his tribe.

Over the years, as he grew older, he started to venture into his village and beyond, to see cities and people who were not familiar with his ways of life. The thirteen trips transformed him. He knew that he had to protect his people, but sometimes that meant going outside their borders.

Entry 02 : Ubunye Kept by T. Nkosi

The Village That Raised Me

The Zulu are a proud and powerful people known for their strong warriors, close family bonds, and deep respect for their ancestors. Our society values strength, courage, loyalty, and protecting their community. Generations of warriors have defended their homeland, passing their traditions and knowledge down to the next generation.

Entry 03 : Past The Border Kept by T. Nkosi

What The Cities Taught Me

On my journey, I met a young boy named Yukio Gray. He was very friendly to me, my ways, and my traditions. He showed me around as I traveled, and eventually, I considered him my first friend, showing that life outside of my tribe is not all so evil and untrustworthy. As of now, he's the only person outside of my tribe to earn my trust.

Entry 04 : Who I Am Now Kept by T. Nkosi

Guarded, Not Cold

I don't warm up to people very quickly, and I can be pretty cautious around people I don't know. However, I have the mindset of a king, so I'm willing to work alongside others and put my personal feelings aside if it means helping my people in the long run. I understand that I can't protect my people by myself, and sometimes I have to trust others and work with them to do what is best for my tribe.
